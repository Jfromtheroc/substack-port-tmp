That is a targeting problem, and it is exactly what geotargeted advertising solves.</p><p>We are The Profound Agency, a visibility agency in Hillsborough, New Jersey. Political campaigns are part of our client roster, and our geotargeted mobile channel is one of the sharpest tools they have: display and video ads served by where someone has physically been.</p><h2>Two jobs: persuade and turn out</h2><p>Every campaign has two audiences. The persuadable, voters who could go either way and need a reason. And the base, supporters who need a nudge to actually vote. They need different messages and they live in different places. Persuasion targets the undecided where they gather. Turnout targets your people with the logistics: where, when, how. One channel serves both, with different fences and different creative.</p><h2>The campaign calendar</h2><p>Early on, the job is name recognition: geofence the events, the downtowns, the places where voters congregate, and introduce the candidate. Mid campaign, it is contrast: conquest the opponent’s events and reach their attendees while the comparison is fresh. Late, it is turnout: fence the early voting sites and remind your voters, daily, that the window is open. The same tool, three phases, three messages.</p><h2>Three plays from the field</h2><p><strong>Geofence the moments.</strong> Draw a boundary around a rally, a town hall, a county fair, an early voting site, and reach the phones inside it. The most politically engaged people in the district, identified by where they chose to be.</p><p><strong>Conquest the opponent’s events.</strong> When the rival holds a rally, their attendees’ location history becomes your audience. Your message reaches them while the event is fresh.</p><p><strong>Measure the ground game.</strong> Footfall attribution reports whether the people you reached later showed up: at your events, your offices, the polls. The ad connects to a real visit, not just a click.</p><h2>Down-ballot budgets</h2><p>You do not need a Senate war chest. Because campaigns are priced per effort, not by tier, a county or municipal race can run a tight geotargeted program around the places that decide it: a few key precincts, the early voting locations, the opponent’s fundraisers. Small geography, small budget, same precision the big races use.</p><p>Picture it. It is the final weekend. Your volunteers are canvassing the neighborhoods that always vote, but the election will be decided by the people who sometimes vote. Your ads find them: the phones that were at the farmers market, the high school football game, the early voting line. Every impression lands on someone whose location history says they show up.</p><p>Demographics guess. Location knows.</p><h2>The message matrix</h2><p>Persuasion and turnout need different creative. The persuadable voter needs contrast and biography: who the candidate is, what the choice means, why it matters. The base voter needs logistics and urgency: where to vote, when it closes, that their vote is the one that decides it. Same channel, different fences, different ads. Campaigns that run one message to both audiences waste half their impressions. The targeting can tell them apart. The creative should too.</p><h2>Earned plus paid</h2><p>Every campaign event is two events: the one the press covers and the one the geofence captures. The rally makes the news. The phones in the crowd become an audience you can reach for weeks. Smart campaigns never hold an event without fencing it, because the crowd is the most valuable list they will ever build: self-selected, politically engaged, geographically concentrated. The event ends. The audience keeps working.</p><h2>The final 72 hours</h2><p>Late in the race, persuasion stops and turnout takes over entirely. The fences tighten to early voting sites and high-propensity neighborhoods. The message simplifies to logistics: vote now, here is where, here is when. Frequency goes up, because the cost of a missed voter dwarfs the cost of one more impression. This is where footfall attribution earns its keep: you can see, in near real time, whether your people are showing up, and shift effort to where they are not.</p><h2>After election day</h2><p>The audience does not expire when the polls close.
